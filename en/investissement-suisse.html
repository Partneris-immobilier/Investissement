Swiss market

Investing in rental real estate in Switzerland

A mature, stable market known for its legal security — but governed by specific rules for foreign buyers and more modest yields than elsewhere.

Overview

A safe-haven market, with low yield but strong appreciation

Switzerland has an average gross rental yield of around 2.9%, among the lowest in the world. This figure is explained by high prices per m² (up to CHF 13,000/m² in the most sought-after areas) and strong demand for security rather than pure performance. In return, the Swiss market offers macroeconomic stability, a strong currency and a legal framework among the most predictable in the world.

Here, yield must be read alongside long-term appreciation and taxation, rather than as immediate cash flow.

Key takeaway

Mortgage interest and maintenance costs are deductible from taxable income in Switzerland — a tax lever that meaningfully improves net yield, particularly when financing with credit.

Legal framework

Lex Koller: what's changing in 2026

The federal law on the acquisition of real estate by persons abroad strictly governs non-residents' access to the Swiss market. The reform that took effect in 2026 tightens these rules further.

Primary residence

Nationals of countries outside the EU/EFTA must now obtain formal cantonal authorization to acquire their primary residence.

Resale obligation

A foreign owner leaving Switzerland has a two-year window to resell their property.

Commercial real estate

Acquisition is now limited to own operational use — pure commercial rental investment is heavily restricted.

Second homes

Cantonal quotas are reduced, including for resales between foreigners who already own property.

Real estate funds

Indirect investments via publicly listed funds become partially restricted for non-residents.

EU/EFTA nationals

Thanks to bilateral agreements, the impact of the reform remains minimal for EU/EFTA citizens residing in Switzerland.

Cross-border commuters (G permit)

Access to second homes and real estate funds is reduced for holders of a cross-border commuter permit. Legal support is strongly recommended before any commitment.

Financing

Swiss mortgage lending

Down payment and equity

A minimum down payment of 20% is generally required (often more for non-residents, up to 30-40%). At least 10% must come from "hard" equity (excluding 2nd pillar pension funds).

Rates and amortization

Swiss mortgage rates remain among the lowest in Europe. The debt must be amortized down to 65% of the property's value, generally over 15 years.

Debt capacity

The usual rule: annual charges (theoretical interest ~5%, amortization, maintenance) ≤ 33% of the household's gross income.

Tax leverage

Mortgage interest and maintenance costs are deductible from taxable income — an advantage to factor into the calculation of actual net yield.

Summary

Advantages and points of caution

Advantages

  • Legal security and political stability recognized worldwide
  • Strong currency, protection against exchange rate risk for a CHF-based resident
  • Taxation optimizable via deduction of interest and maintenance costs
  • Historically low mortgage rates
  • Solid long-term appreciation in major urban centers

Points of caution

  • Gross yield among the lowest in the world (2.5 – 4.5%)
  • Restricted access for non-residents outside the EU/EFTA (Lex Koller)
  • High entry prices in major centers (Geneva, Zurich)
  • 2026 reform: resale obligation upon leaving Switzerland
  • Rental market regulated according to cantons
