Legal framework

Who can invest where, and under what conditions?

Before any yield, the first question is legal: does your nationality allow you to buy? Do you need a company, a lease, an authorization? Does it open the door to a residence permit? Here is the legal framework, country by country.

Understanding the basics

4 questions to ask before investing abroad

1. Can I buy in full ownership, or are there restrictions?▾

Three broad situations exist around the world:

  • Open access: most of Western Europe, the United States, Japan, Argentina, Uruguay — no distinction between nationals and foreigners.
  • Conditional access: administrative authorization (Austria, Croatia, Cyprus), minimum price threshold (Malaysia), or designated zones (United Arab Emirates, Qatar, Thailand for condominiums).
  • Direct ownership impossible: land remains reserved for nationals (Thailand, Philippines, Vietnam, Romania, Bulgaria) — access then goes through a local company, a long-term lease or a right of use (such as Hak Pakai in Indonesia).
2. Does my nationality or resident status change things?▾

Yes, systematically. Three factors come up most often:

  • Membership of an economic area: EU/EFTA citizens benefit from easier access throughout the European Union (and in Switzerland, subject to residency conditions).
  • Reciprocity agreements: countries such as Italy or Turkey apply reciprocity — if your country allows Italians/Turks to buy at home, you are generally allowed to buy in theirs.
  • Diaspora status: India grants near-free access to its nationals abroad (NRI) and its overseas citizens (OCI), very different from the regime applied to other foreigners.
3. Can the purchase open the door to residence (golden visa)?▾

Many countries link real estate investment to a residence permit, but the rules change quickly:

  • Still active in 2026: Greece (from €250,000), Malta, Hungary, United Arab Emirates, Turkey (citizenship from 400,000 USD), Mauritius, Panama, Costa Rica.
  • Suspended or recently refocused: Portugal (golden visa refocused on funds), Spain (real estate excluded since April 2025), Ireland (suspended since 2023).
  • Never linked to real estate: New Zealand, Canada, United States (EB-5 requires job creation, not a simple rental purchase).

Thresholds and conditions change frequently — we verify the current status before any decision.

4. What legal structure should I plan for?▾

Depending on the country, the acquisition may require:

  • Purchase in your own name: the simplest solution, possible in most open markets.
  • Local company: required in Thailand (49% max), in Romania/Bulgaria for land, or recommended in the United States and the United Kingdom for estate planning optimization.
  • Trust / fideicomiso: mechanism specific to Mexico for the restricted coastal/border zone.
  • Long-term lease or right of use: Vietnam (50 years), Indonesia (Hak Pakai, up to 80 years), Thailand (renewable 30-year lease).
